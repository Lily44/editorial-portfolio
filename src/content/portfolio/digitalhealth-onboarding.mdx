## The Challenge

A clinical training program was drowning students in dense text. New practitioners needed to understand complex, compliance-heavy topics - terminology, best practices, legal requirements, and real-world scenarios - but traditional page layouts were not sticking. Students felt unprepared and overwhelmed navigating multi-topic modules.

## The Solution

I designed the entire 60-minute onboarding as an **interactive, progressive learning experience** using flip cards for terminology, accordion sections for best practices, and scenario-based cards for real-world application. The design transformed compliance training from passive reading into active engagement, making critical concepts memorable and immediately applicable.

**Role:** Designer (UX/UI) + Frontend Developer  
**Collaborators:** Subject Matter Expert (healthcare/telehealth content)  
**Tech Stack:** HTML/CSS/JavaScript (custom)  
**Focus:** Information architecture, interactive patterns, WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility

![Digital Health Onboarding introduction screen](/images/telehealth-intro.png)

## The Impact

Post-launch feedback was overwhelmingly positive. Supervisors reported:

- **Higher engagement** - Students praised the interactive elements and scenario-based approach.
- **Better retention** - Key concepts stuck better than with previous text-heavy materials.
- **Increased confidence** - Students felt equipped to handle real telehealth situations.
- **Strong adoption** - The module became standard in the training program with high completion rates.

The project demonstrates how strategic information architecture, thoughtful UX patterns, and accessibility-first development can transform rigid compliance training into engaging learning that sticks.
